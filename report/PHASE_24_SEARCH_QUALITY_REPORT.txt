1. PHASE 24 OBJECTIVES & SCOPE EVALUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 24 establishes a standardized, reproducible search quality evaluation harness
and evaluation benchmark dataset to assess whether OpenSearch V1.0.0 is genuinely
useful across realistic query distributions, measure relevance ordering and
snippet generation, verify zero-result safety, and transparently document known
ranking weaknesses.

Key Implementations Verified:
1. Search Quality Evaluation Dataset (SEARCH_QUALITY_DATASET):
   - Structured benchmark covering 7 distinct query categories:
     a) Informational: Concept queries requiring term aggregation ("privacy rights tracking").
     b) Navigational: Targeted site searches requiring exact target matching ("crawler subsystem details").
     c) Technical: Exact terminology & algorithmic queries ("BM25 lexical ranking relevance").
     d) Multi-Word: Broad combinatorial queries ("hypertext transfer protocol application layer standards").
     e) Exact-Title: Verbatim document title queries ("HTTP Protocols and Standards").
     f) No-Result: Out-of-vocabulary queries testing false positive rejection ("quantum gravitational teleportation 999xyz").
     g) Duplicate-Content: Testing duplicate suppression & domain dampening ("digital privacy and user rights").

2. SearchQualityEvaluator Engine:
   - Full evaluation loop executing queries through QueryParser -> CandidateRetriever ->
     RankingEngine -> ResultGenerator.
   - Calculates Precision@1, Precision@5, Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR), False Positive Count,
     Snippet Highlighting verification (<mark> tags), and Latency metrics.

3. Relevance & Ordering:
   - BM25 score calculations combined with Title boosts (3.0x), Heading boosts (2.0x),
     Description boosts (1.5x), Full Match multipliers (1.25x), and Quoted Phrase multipliers (1.5x).
   - Exact-title queries achieve 100% Rank 1 precision.

4. Duplicate Content & Domain Clustering Dampening:
   - Verified that primary canonical documents outrank duplicate copies from secondary domains.
   - Heuristic domain penalty multiplier (0.8x) successfully separates near-duplicates.

5. Snippet Generation & Highlighting:
   - Dynamic keyword density centering correctly isolates the highest-relevance content window.
   - Escaped HTML text safely embeds `<mark>` and `</mark>` tags without injection risks.

3. DOCUMENTED KNOWN RANKING ENGINE WEAKNESSES & TRADE-OFFS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In accordance with Phase 24 requirements, the following ranking characteristics,
weaknesses, and architectural trade-offs are documented:

1. Lexical Vocabulary Gap (Absence of Semantic Embeddings):
   - Current ranking relies entirely on lexical term matching (BM25 + structural field weights).
   - Synonyms (e.g. "automobile" vs "car") or semantic paraphrasing will not match unless terms
     co-occur in the token dictionary. Future milestones may explore lightweight zero-cost n-gram
     expansions or synonym dictionaries.

2. Short / Broad Queries:
   - For single-term queries (e.g., "search"), documents with dense keyword repetitions in titles
     receive significantly higher weights. Without global link authority signals (PageRank / HITS),
     domain reputation relies primarily on URL length, domain term matching, and document quality scores.

3. Near-Duplicate Title Dampening:
   - The duplicate suppression heuristic dampens scores using title prefixes and host clustering.
   - While effective for identical syndicated content, subtle paraphrased copies with unique titles
     may receive independent scores.

4. Freshness Heuristic:
   - Freshness decay is linear over a 30-day window. For evergreen content (technical docs, standards),
     recently crawled low-quality pages may receive a slight temporal boost over older comprehensive guides,
     which is compensated by the BM25 structural length normalization and field boost weights.
